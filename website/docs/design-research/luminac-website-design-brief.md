# Luminac Website Design Brief

Date: 2026-06-02  
Project: `luminac.net` rebuild  
Status: Design direction locked for text-first planning  
Primary audience: Architects and interior designers  
Secondary audience: Dealers and retailers

## Purpose

This brief defines how the public Luminac website should look, feel, and behave before visual mockups or code begin. It translates the approved planning direction into a design-ready foundation for homepage structure, catalogue browsing, product pages, trust content, and animation limits.

The website should present Luminac as a premium Indian architectural lighting brand built on trust, technical clarity, and atmospheric design.

## Core Positioning

Luminac should feel like a premium lighting partner for serious projects, not a generic ecommerce catalogue.

The site must communicate:

- Premium Indian architectural lighting.
- Trust and transparency.
- Product seriousness and technical clarity.
- Design sensitivity for architects and interior designers.
- Practical catalogue usability for dealers and retailers.
- Project support through enquiry, specification help, and gated technical resources.

The design should avoid fake luxury, generic stock imagery, unsupported claims, and over-styled motion that slows down product discovery.

## Visual Identity: Warm Precision

The chosen design identity is **Warm Precision**.

Warm Precision means:

- Warm, restrained, premium, and architectural.
- Clean enough for technical product browsing.
- Atmospheric enough to make lighting feel emotional and spatial.
- Transparent enough to support trust and professional decision-making.

Recommended visual language:

- Base colors: warm off-white, soft white, charcoal, graphite, warm grey.
- Accents: restrained champagne, brass, optical amber, and small technical cool accents only where useful.
- Typography: elegant display type for brand moments, readable sans-serif for navigation, specs, filters, and product information.
- Layout: generous whitespace, thin dividers, clear alignment, restrained cards, and editorial section rhythm.
- Imagery: real product images, CAD or line drawings, application photos, and real project photos where available.
- Motion: light-driven reveals, subtle parallax, soft spotlight sweeps, and scroll-triggered product focus.

Do not make the site feel like:

- A black-heavy nightclub or cyberpunk lighting site.
- A beige lifestyle template.
- A generic ecommerce grid.
- A slow animation demo where product access is delayed.

## Audience Priorities

### Architects And Interior Designers

The design should help this audience understand atmosphere, product fit, technical credibility, and project support.

They need:

- Premium brand confidence.
- Application context.
- Product families and subcategories.
- Clear specs.
- CAD or line drawing previews.
- Datasheets and technical asset request paths.
- Project and About Us proof.
- Easy enquiry for specification support.

### Dealers And Retailers

The design should keep catalogue lookup fast and predictable.

They need:

- Indoor and Outdoor catalogue structure.
- Subcategory pages.
- Model-code search.
- Product grids or lists with key specs.
- Quick access to product details.
- Enquiry/contact paths.

Homeowners and end clients can still use the site, but they are not the design priority for the first full design direction.

## Content Rule

The launch design must use verified content only.

Allowed:

- Real products.
- Real model codes.
- Real specifications.
- Real product images.
- Real CAD or line drawing previews.
- Real application images.
- Real projects or case studies.
- Verified company, process, quality, support, warranty, and contact information.

Not allowed:

- Fake projects.
- Fake certifications.
- Fake testimonials.
- Stock-heavy brand proof.
- Unsupported "industry leading" style claims.
- Placeholder content that the visual design depends on.

If real content is missing, the design should include a clean future-ready section or omit the section until content exists.

## Site Structure

### Home

The homepage should sell the brand story first, reveal selected products second, and open the full catalogue third.

Recommended flow:

1. Brand storytelling hero.
2. Controlled spotlight product reveal.
3. Trust and transparency section.
4. Projects or application proof.
5. Catalogue gateway with Indoor and Outdoor entry points.
6. Featured subcategories.
7. Project enquiry call to action.

The first viewport should not be a search bar or product grid. It should explain Luminac's identity and credibility while giving users a clear path deeper into products.

### Catalogue

Catalogue browsing should be category-first and subcategory-first.

Primary structure:

- Indoor.
- Outdoor.
- Subcategories under each environment.
- Product lists inside each subcategory.
- Search as a support tool, not the main information architecture.

Search should support model-code lookup and product discovery, but users should be able to browse naturally through categories without typing.

### Subcategory Pages

Each subcategory page should act like a professional range overview.

Required content:

- Subcategory name and short purpose statement.
- Product list or grid.
- Key specs visible at card/list level.
- Strong product images.
- Filter controls where useful.
- Links to product detail pages.
- Related subcategories.
- Project enquiry call to action.

Subcategory pages should feel calmer and more technical than the homepage. They should not rely on heavy scroll animation.

### Product Detail Pages

Each product page should be a professional spec page.

Required sections:

- Product name and model code.
- Category and subcategory.
- Primary product image.
- Application photos where available.
- Key specs.
- Finish/body color information.
- CCT, beam angle, wattage, light source, IP rating, and other available technical details.
- CAD or line drawing preview.
- Datasheet or technical document area.
- Gated CAD/download request.
- Project enquiry call to action.
- Related products from the same subcategory or family.

MRP/pricing should not be public in the first design direction. Commercial questions should route through project enquiry or sales contact.

### About Us

About Us should be a full credibility page, not a short brand paragraph.

It should cover:

- Company story.
- Luminac's positioning as a premium Indian lighting brand.
- Values and design philosophy.
- Transparency about process and support.
- Quality approach.
- Project support promise.
- Team, founder, showroom, manufacturing, or operations information if verified.
- Contact paths for architects, designers, dealers, and project buyers.

### Projects

Projects should launch with a small number of real projects or application stories.

If only a few real projects are available, design the section as a focused proof layer rather than a large portfolio. Each project can include:

- Project name or type.
- Location if approved.
- Application area.
- Product families used.
- Real photos.
- Short design/support note.

Do not fake a large portfolio. Leave room for future case-study expansion.

### Contact And Enquiry

The main conversion action is **Project Enquiry**.

The contact experience should support:

- Project enquiry.
- Product specification help.
- CAD or technical asset request.
- Dealer or business enquiry.
- General contact.

The site should feel like Luminac helps users choose and specify lighting, not only browse products.

## Navigation

The top navigation should balance brand and catalogue.

Recommended primary links:

- Products or Catalogue.
- Projects.
- About Us.
- Contact.

Recommended support actions:

- Search.
- Project Enquiry.
- Technical asset request where contextually relevant.

Navigation should make products easy to reach without making the first impression feel catalogue-only.

## Trust And Transparency Strategy

Trust should be integrated everywhere.

Homepage:

- Brand story.
- Real project/application proof.
- Quality and support message.
- Clear path to catalogue and enquiry.

Product pages:

- Verified specs.
- Real assets.
- CAD or line drawing previews.
- Transparent technical request flow.
- Related products and enquiry support.

About Us:

- Full credibility story.
- Values, process, support, and verified company details.

Projects:

- Real work only.
- Small but credible launch structure.

Contact:

- Clear paths for architects, designers, dealers, and technical requests.

## Animation Direction

Motion should be signature but restrained.

Allowed:

- Hero lighting atmosphere.
- Controlled scroll reveal for selected products.
- Subtle spotlight sweeps.
- Product focus transitions.
- Light parallax.
- Refined hover states.
- Navigation overlay transitions.

Avoid:

- Long forced scroll narratives.
- Heavy 3D throughout the site.
- Animation that hides product access.
- Blank transition states.
- Layout shifts during scroll.
- Motion that makes the catalogue feel slow.

Performance rules:

- Heavy animation belongs only in the hero and curated reveal sections.
- Catalogue pages, subcategory pages, product pages, and search should remain lightweight.
- Provide reduced-motion behavior.
- Design should still work if 3D assets fail or are disabled.
- Text and product content must remain readable and accessible without animation.

## Product And Asset Policy

Product pages should show CAD or line drawing previews publicly.

Technical downloads should be gated or request-based by default:

- CAD files.
- IES/LDT files.
- Detailed technical packages.
- Dealer-only resources.

Public resources can include:

- Product images.
- Application photos.
- Core specs.
- CAD or line drawing previews.
- Public datasheets if business policy allows.

This balances transparency for architects with lead control and technical asset safety.

## Content Inventory Needed Before Visual Design

Before high-fidelity visual design, collect or confirm:

- Final category and subcategory tree.
- Product model list.
- Product images.
- Application photos.
- CAD or line drawing previews.
- Datasheets and technical files.
- Company story.
- Founder/team/company facts.
- Quality/process/support facts.
- Warranty or service policy if it will be shown.
- Real project photos and descriptions.
- Contact details and enquiry routing.
- Logo files and brand marks.
- Any verified certifications, standards, or partner claims.

Missing content should be treated as an open gap, not filled with fake premium language.

## Design Acceptance Criteria

The design direction is successful if:

- First-time visitors understand Luminac as a premium Indian architectural lighting brand.
- Architects and designers can reach specs, CAD previews, application imagery, and enquiry support quickly.
- Dealers and retailers can browse Indoor/Outdoor subcategories and search by model code.
- The homepage feels story-led without blocking product discovery.
- Product pages feel professional and credible.
- Trust appears across Home, About, Projects, Catalogue, and Product pages.
- The site remains usable with reduced motion or failed 3D assets.
- Public pages do not show unverified claims, fake projects, or fake trust markers.

## Next Design Work

The next non-coding design steps are:

1. Finalize the public category and subcategory tree.
2. Create a content inventory from real Luminac assets.
3. Define the exact homepage section copy and hierarchy.
4. Define the product page information hierarchy in detail.
5. Produce text-only wireframes for Home, Catalogue, Subcategory, Product, About, Projects, and Contact.
6. Only after that, move to visual mockups or frontend implementation.
